I am currently exploring underexplored parts of the training stack that enhance the capabilities models acquire during training. I want to understand ML on both a theoretical and practical level, which means reading papers, implementing ideas, and occasionally discovering why something that sounds elegant falls apart in practice. My most recent endeavors include:

- [Temporal Regularized Learning](/work/) by **Davide Wiest**. A local, self-supervised learning procedure that is local in time and space [2025] [Code](https://github.com/Axym-Labs/TRL) [Paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18673107)
- [pptrain](/work/) — a PyTorch library for pre-pretraining language models on synthetic tasks [2026] [Code](https://github.com/Axym-Labs/pptrain)
- [Closed-Form Initialization](/work/) — exploring whether neural networks can be initialized analytically [2026] [Code](https://github.com/Axym-Labs/closed-form-initialization)
- [Gradient Routing for Continual Learning](/work/) — testing whether sparsity can reduce catastrophic forgetting [2026] [Code](https://github.com/Axym-Labs/gradient-routing-continual-learning)

Outside of work, I go on long walks when I am stuck on a problem or visit the library for inspiration. Philosophically I lean toward anti-essentialism and structuralism, with bundle theory as a interesting framing.
