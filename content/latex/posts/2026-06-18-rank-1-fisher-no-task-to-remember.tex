\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23593}{Avoid Catastrophic Forgetting with Rank-1 Fisher from Diffusion Models} proposes EWC with a near rank-1 empirical Fisher observed at low diffusion SNR. The theorem, algorithm, and continual-learning interpretation do not describe the same object: the low-SNR limit is task-independent, the algorithm averages over all timesteps, the linear surrogate lacks a nonlinear stability argument, and the true Fisher in the analyzed Gaussian regime is isotropic.

\section{What EWC must preserve}\label{what-ewc-must-preserve}

EWC adds an old-task quadratic penalty to the new-task loss,

\[
L_B(\theta)+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\theta-\theta_A^\star)^\top F_A(\theta-\theta_A^\star).
\]

In its Bayesian derivation, \(F_A\) approximates local old-task posterior curvature (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796}{Kirkpatrick et al.}). With \(F_A\approx\lambda_1vv^\top\), only movement along \(v\) is protected. A continual-learning justification therefore requires \(v\) to encode old-task sensitivity; a task-independent direction is merely a generic stabilization penalty.

\section{Low SNR removes the task}\label{low-snr-removes-the-task}

For the variance-preserving forward process,

\[
x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,
\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),
\]

low SNR means \(x_t\approx\epsilon\). The optimal score and noise-prediction target are then dominated by

\[
s_t^\star(x_t)\approx-\frac{x_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t},
\qquad
\epsilon^\star(x_t,t)\approx\frac{x_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}.
\]

These leading terms depend on the noise schedule, not the old data distribution. Task information occurs only in lower-order corrections as SNR rises. The low-SNR rank-1 direction thus describes a universal Gaussian denoising scale, not an old-task fact.

The implementation restores task information by averaging gradients over the joint data--timestep sampling process. Mid- and high-SNR denoising depends on data geometry, but it lies outside the clean low-SNR derivation. The theorem supplies rank 1 where the task disappears; the all-timestep algorithm restores the task by leaving that regime. Distillation may also preserve the old task, but it is a separate mechanism.

\section{Linearity does not survive automatically}\label{linearity-does-not-survive-automatically}

Let a neural-network gradient decompose into a linear-surrogate term and nonlinear remainder,

\[
g(x)=g_{\mathrm{lin}}(x)+e(x).
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb E[gg^\top]
=G_{\mathrm{lin}}+R,
\quad
R=\mathbb E[g_{\mathrm{lin}}e^\top+eg_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top+ee^\top].
\]

The leading eigenspace is stable only when, for instance,

\[
\lVert R\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\ll
\lambda_1(G_{\mathrm{lin}})-\lambda_2(G_{\mathrm{lin}}).
\]

Output-space proximity to a scaling map does not establish parameter-gradient collinearity after the pullback through a U-Net. The paper neither bounds this residual relative to the eigengap nor measures it.

\section{The true Fisher points the other way}\label{the-true-fisher-points-the-other-way}

For the conditional Gaussian model

\[
x\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_x),
\qquad y\mid x,A\sim\mathcal N(Ax,\sigma^2I_m),
\]

the likelihood score is \(\nabla_A\log p_A(y\mid x)=\sigma^{-2}(y-Ax)x^\top\), giving

\[
F_{\mathrm{true}}=\frac{1}{\sigma^2}\Sigma_x\otimes I_m.
\]

At low SNR, \(\Sigma_x=\tau^2I_d\), hence

\[
F_{\mathrm{true}}=\frac{\tau^2}{\sigma^2}I_{dm}.
\]

No rank-1 direction is preferred: every Frobenius-norm perturbation of \(A\) has the same local predictive KL cost. The paper's rank-1 matrix is an empirical second moment of surrogate-loss gradients, not Fisher-information geometry. As \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12558}{Kunstner, Balles, and Hennig} emphasize, the empirical Fisher is not generally an empirical estimate of the Fisher and need not represent curvature.

\section{The decisive tests}\label{the-decisive-tests}

The method needs timestep-restricted comparisons: low-SNR, mid-SNR, high-SNR, and all-timestep matrices under the same replay and distillation setup. These would show where old-task preservation enters. The neural claim also needs the nonlinear residual and empirical eigengap. Finally, the matrix should be named and analyzed as a surrogate-gradient second moment, not a true Fisher; an optimization-preservation derivation such as \href{/files/improved-elastic-weight-consolidation.pdf}{IEWC} supplies the appropriate alternative framework.

The \href{https://openreview.net/forum?id=zCZcbRsc4g}{OpenReview page} lists the paper as an ICLR 2026 poster. The central chain nevertheless remains unaligned: its theorem removes task identity, while its working algorithm depends on task-dependent timesteps outside that theorem.
