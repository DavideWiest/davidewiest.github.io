Prepretraining first trains a language model on synthetic or abstract data, then transfers it to natural text. The empirical claim is that the synthetic phase produces a better initialization. A shared \href{https://github.com/Axym-Labs/pptrain}{\texttt{pptrain}} evaluation tested six proposed mechanisms---NCA sequences, LIME reasoning, simpler symbolic tasks, procedural programs, Dyck languages, and synthetic summarization---on a Pythia-410M architecture with three seeds (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10055}{Lee et al.~2026}; \href{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wu21c.html}{Wu et al.~2021}; \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10139}{Wu et al.~2022}; \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21725}{Jiang et al.~2026}; \href{https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.273/}{Krishna et al.~2021}).

Under this protocol, five synthetic methods finished worse than random initialization. Simpler tasks improved final evaluation loss by \(0.16\%\). A natural-text warmup using the upstream budget beat all six, and the reasoning and algorithmic probes showed no transfer gain.

\section{Protocol}\label{protocol}

The model used the Pythia-410M architecture and tokenizer, a 12,288-token context, public streamed text datasets, and separate warmup, train, and evaluation slices (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01373}{Biderman et al.~2023}). Every task compared three conditions per seed: downstream training from random initialization; synthetic upstream training followed by downstream transfer; and an upstream natural-text warmup followed by the same downstream phase. The shared public-data setup tests transfer under one controlled proxy protocol rather than reproducing every original corpus and budget.

The loss curves preserved this ordering through training, not only at the endpoint. NCA also failed its upstream task: held-out next-patch token accuracy was \(0.0024\%\), downstream CE was \(6.779\) versus \(6.232\) from scratch and \(6.044\) after natural-text warmup, and effective rank fell to \(4.6\) versus \(48.4\) from scratch. Reference-parity fixtures rule out a changed generator as the explanation.

\section{Interpretation}\label{interpretation}

The controls locate two failures. Random initialization tests whether synthetic training helps at all; a natural-text warmup tests whether synthetic data is the best use of the same upstream phase. Synthetic transfer fails the first comparison in five tasks and the second in all six. Stiffening under covariate shift is one compatible mechanism: warm starts can generalize worse than retraining, networks can lose plasticity, and shifted fine-tuning can distort useful features (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08475}{Ash and Adams 2020}; \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812}{Dohare et al.~2023}; \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054}{Kumar et al.~2022}).

Under this Pythia-410M public-data protocol, synthetic prepretraining does not provide a useful general initialization. Future claims need three controls: beat random initialization, beat an equal upstream budget of natural text, and verify that the synthetic task was learned.
